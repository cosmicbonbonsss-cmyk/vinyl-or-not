    About the walnut look

    Walnut is a rich medium-to-dark brown with a soft, flowing grain and a gray or purple undertone. It feels warm and upscale. It shows dust, lint, and light pet hair more than mid-tone floors, but hides darker dirt. It pairs with white, greige, or deep green walls and brass or black hardware, and it needs good light in a small room.

    Styling walnut in a 12 by 10 living room

    Walnut suits cream, camel, or green sofas, and a lighter rug keeps the room from feeling heavy.

    Living room notes

    Living rooms take furniture weight, sunlight, and foot traffic from the front door. Put felt pads under sofa and chair legs, and use rug pads that are labeled safe for vinyl, since some rubber or latex backings can stain or discolor the wear layer. Strong sun through large windows can fade or heat planks over time, so blinds or UV film help. In an open plan, check the manufacturer's maximum run length; long runs into a hallway or dining room often need a T-molding at the doorway.

    Walnut in a 12 by 10 living room: tips

    • Walnut shows dust and light pet hair in sunlit spots, so consider where the sun lands when you place rugs.
    • Run the planks toward the main window or along the longest wall, and continue the same direction into connected rooms for a seamless look.
    • Walnut makes white trim and baseboards pop, so freshen the paint on the living room trim before the new floor goes in.

    What to buy for this living room

    • Core: rigid core (SPC or WPC); WPC feels a little softer and quieter underfoot
    • Wear layer: 12 mil for most homes; 20 mil with dogs or a busy entry path
    • Thickness: about 5–8 mm total, ideally with an attached pad
    • Waste: 10% for a straight lay; more if the room has angled walls or a fireplace hearth
    • Finish: matte finish; gloss shows footprints and dust on darker floors
