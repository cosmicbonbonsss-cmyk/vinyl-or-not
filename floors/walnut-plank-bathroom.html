    About the walnut plank look

    Walnut plank is a darker, more uniform walnut look with long boards and few knots, so the floor reads as calm, even lines. Like other dark floors, it shows dust and lint, and scratches in the wear layer tend to show as pale lines. It pairs with white or light walls, mid-century furniture, and brass or black accents.

    Styling walnut plank in a 5 by 8 bathroom

    Walnut plank looks good with white tile, a floating white or wood vanity, and matte black fixtures.

    Bathroom notes

    A bathroom needs a waterproof rigid core plank; SPC is the usual pick. Waterproof planks still let water reach the subfloor at the edges, so leave the expansion gap around the tub, toilet flange, and vanity, then seal it in the wet zones with 100% silicone caulk. Pull the toilet, run planks under its footprint, and reset it with a new wax ring. Fit planks around a heavy built-in vanity rather than under it. In a small room the cuts around the toilet and door jamb take most of the time.

    Walnut plank in a 5 by 8 bathroom: tips

    • Walnut plank hides dark hair but shows dust, powder, and dried water spots, so keep a microfiber mop handy.
    • The even grain keeps a small bathroom looking tidy and uncluttered.
    • Long walnut planks look best running toward the main light source or the longest wall of the bathroom, which also keeps the seams less obvious.

    What to buy for this bathroom

    • Core: waterproof rigid core; SPC is the usual choice for bathrooms
    • Wear layer: 12 mil; 20 mil if dogs use the room
    • Thickness: about 5–6 mm total; enough for a bathroom while keeping the toilet flange and door clearance simple
    • Waste: 10%; in a 5 by 8 room you'll buy whole boxes, so the spare covers it
    • Finish: matte finish; gloss shows footprints and dust on darker floors
