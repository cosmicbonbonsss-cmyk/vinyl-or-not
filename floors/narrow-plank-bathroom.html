    About the narrow plank look

    Narrow plank means thin boards, roughly 3 to 5 inches wide, which gives the busier, traditional look of older strip floors. The extra seams break up the surface and help hide scuffs, and running the planks along the long wall makes a small room look longer. The trade-off is more planks to click together, so the install takes longer.

    Styling narrow plank in a 5 by 8 bathroom

    Narrow planks pair with white tile and a traditional vanity and fit around tight spots with less waste.

    Bathroom notes

    A bathroom needs a waterproof rigid core plank; SPC is the usual pick. Waterproof planks still let water reach the subfloor at the edges, so leave the expansion gap around the tub, toilet flange, and vanity, then seal it in the wet zones with 100% silicone caulk. Pull the toilet, run planks under its footprint, and reset it with a new wax ring. Fit planks around a heavy built-in vanity rather than under it. In a small room the cuts around the toilet and door jamb take most of the time.

    Narrow plank in a 5 by 8 bathroom: tips

    • Narrow plank keeps a small bathroom bright while hiding dust and most stray hairs; only dark hair stands out near the sink.
    • Narrow planks suit a 5 by 8 bathroom well, since they fit around the toilet and vanity with less waste than wide boards.
    • Narrow planks change the plank count a lot: set the plank width in the measure tool (for example 4 inches instead of 7) so the bathroom estimate is realistic.

    What to buy for this bathroom

    • Core: waterproof rigid core; SPC is the usual choice for bathrooms
    • Wear layer: 12 mil; 20 mil if dogs use the room
    • Thickness: about 5–6 mm total; enough for a bathroom while keeping the toilet flange and door clearance simple
    • Waste: 10%; in a 5 by 8 room you'll buy whole boxes, so the spare covers it
    • Finish: matte or low-gloss finish; embossed grain makes the look more realistic
