    About the natural pine look

    Natural pine is yellow-orange with bold knots and wide grain, a cottage or cabin look. The knots and color variation hide dirt and scratches. Real pine dents easily, but LVP with a decent wear layer doesn't share that weakness, so you get the look without the softness. It pairs with white, sage, and soft blue walls.

    Styling natural pine in a 5 by 8 bathroom

    Natural pine looks good with white beadboard or tile, a white or blue vanity, and simple brass or nickel fixtures.

    Bathroom notes

    A bathroom needs a waterproof rigid core plank; SPC is the usual pick. Waterproof planks still let water reach the subfloor at the edges, so leave the expansion gap around the tub, toilet flange, and vanity, then seal it in the wet zones with 100% silicone caulk. Pull the toilet, run planks under its footprint, and reset it with a new wax ring. Fit planks around a heavy built-in vanity rather than under it. In a small room the cuts around the toilet and door jamb take most of the time.

    Natural pine in a 5 by 8 bathroom: tips

    • Natural pine warms up a small white bathroom without showing every hair the way very dark floors do.
    • In a small bathroom, a busy grain can compete with patterned tile or shower walls, so keep the other surfaces simple.
    • Pine-look planks have repeating knots, so in the bathroom check that two identical planks don't end up side by side before you click them in.

    What to buy for this bathroom

    • Core: waterproof rigid core; SPC is the usual choice for bathrooms
    • Wear layer: 12 mil; 20 mil if dogs use the room
    • Thickness: about 4–6 mm total; thinner planks keep the toilet flange and door clearance simple
    • Waste: 10%; in a 5 by 8 room you'll buy whole boxes, so the spare covers it
    • Finish: matte or low-gloss finish; embossed grain makes the look more realistic
