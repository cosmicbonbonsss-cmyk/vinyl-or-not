    How to cut vinyl plank flooring

    Most straight cuts on rigid-core vinyl plank are score and snap: a sharp utility knife, a straightedge, and a bend. A plank cutter speeds that up. Save the saws for notches, holes, curves, and door jambs. Here is which tool goes with which cut.

    Before you cut

    • Read your install sheet. It lists the cutting methods and blades the plank maker allows, the expansion gap, and the shortest piece allowed. Many say about 8 in.
    • Mark the face. Use a pencil and a square. Measure twice, and remember the gap at the wall.
    • Test on a scrap. Check the face and the locking edge for chips before you cut a full plank.
    • Put the cut edge at the wall. Trim or baseboard covers it. The factory edge locks to the next plank.

    Score and snap (most straight cuts)

    1. Mark — draw the line on the face of the plank with a square or straightedge.
    2. Score — hold the straightedge firmly and run a sharp utility knife along it several times. You are cutting a deep groove, not going all the way through.
    3. Snap — hold one side down and lift the other, close to the score. The plank breaks along the line.
    4. Finish from the back — fold the plank and cut through the attached pad or backing with the knife.

    Long rips work the same way, but they take more passes and a steady straightedge. Change blades often. A dull blade wanders and chips the face.

    Cutters and saws

    Vinyl plank guillotine

    Quick, quiet, dust-free square cuts. Check the cutter's thickness rating against your plank. Thick rigid planks may be past its limit.

    Use: lots of square end cuts on a bigger job.

    Miter saw

    Fast, repeatable crosscuts and angles. Clamp the plank, use a fine blade made for vinyl or composite flooring, and control dust.

    Use: many crosscuts or angled cuts.

    Circular saw

    Long rips when scoring is slow. Support the plank fully, clamp a straightedge as a guide, and watch for kickback.

    Use: long straight rips and thick planks.

    Jigsaw

    Notches, curves, and cutouts. Use a fine-tooth blade (one install guide calls for carbide) and keep the shoe flat so the face doesn't chip.

    Use: around cabinets, vents, and odd shapes.

    Oscillating multi-tool

    The usual DIY pick for undercutting door jambs and trimming tight notches without a full saw setup.

    Use: door jambs and small notches.

    Hole saw or spade bit

    Clean round holes for pipes. Size it larger than the pipe so the floor keeps its expansion gap.

    Use: radiator, sink, and toilet supply pipes.

    Cutting around doors, vents, and pipes

    Door jambs: cut the jamb, not the plank

    1. Make a height guide — lay a scrap plank, with any underlayment, on the subfloor against the jamb.
    2. Undercut — rest a jamb saw, handsaw, or oscillating tool flat on the scrap and cut through the wood casing and jamb.
    3. Clean out — vacuum the sawdust. Leftover dust under the casing becomes a bump.
    4. Slide the plank under — the cut edge and expansion gap stay hidden beneath the casing.
    Metal door frames: install sheets say not to undercut them. Scribe the plank around the frame, keep the expansion gap, and fill it with the sealant your sheet names.

    Floor vents

    Lift the register cover. Lay the plank in place and mark the opening, or make a paper template. Drill a starter hole inside the waste area at each corner, cut between them with a jigsaw, and test-fit before the plank locks in. The register cover hides the cut edge.

    Pipes

    1. Mark the center — set the plank against its neighbor and mark where the pipe lands, or trace a paper template.
    2. Drill oversized — many sheets want about 1/4–3/8 in of space around pipes, so drill a bit larger than the pipe.
    3. Split it to fit — if the plank can't slide over the pipe, cut through the hole to the edge, fit both pieces around the pipe, and lock them in.
    4. Cover the gap — a pipe collar or the sealant your sheet names, never glue that pins the floating floor.

    The last row

    Lay the plank to cut exactly on top of the last full row. Lay another plank on top of it, pushed against the wall (with spacers), and use its edge to mark the width. Rip along the line, and put the cut edge at the wall.

    Safety

    • Safety glasses for every cut, and gloves for sharp edges. Some install sheets also call for a dust mask.
    • Knife: cut away from your body and the hand holding the straightedge. Retract or cap the blade between cuts.
    • Saws: clamp or fully support the plank, keep guards in place, and follow the saw maker's instructions. Cut outside or use dust collection when you can.
    • Old floors: don't sand, saw, or scrape old vinyl or its glue in an older home until it has been tested for asbestos.
    • Clean up: vacuum chips and dust before laying planks. Grit under a floating floor shows.

    How to cut LVP FAQ

    What is the easiest way to cut vinyl plank flooring?

    For straight cuts on most rigid-core planks, score and snap. Mark the face, score it several times with a sharp utility knife along a straightedge or square, bend the plank to snap it along the score, then cut through any attached pad from the back. Many install sheets list this as the standard method.

    Do I need a saw to cut LVP?

    Not for most straight cuts. A utility knife or a plank cutter handles crosscuts and many long rips. A saw helps with notches, curves, holes, lots of repeated cuts, and thick planks that don't snap cleanly. Many install sheets list a jigsaw, circular saw, or miter saw as the fallback.

    How do you cut LVP around a door jamb?

    Undercut the wood jamb and casing instead of notching the plank around them. Lay a scrap plank with its underlayment on the floor as a height guide and cut along its top with a jamb saw, handsaw, or oscillating tool. Then slide the plank under. Never undercut metal door frames; scribe around them and keep the expansion gap.

    How do you cut vinyl plank around a pipe?

    Make a paper template or mark the pipe position on the plank, then drill a hole a bit larger than the pipe so the floor keeps its expansion gap. Many sheets ask for about 1/4 to 3/8 inch of space around pipes. Cover the gap with a pipe collar or the sealant your install sheet names.

    Which side up should I cut vinyl plank?

    Score and snap with the face up. With a power saw, follow the plank maker's and saw maker's instructions, because the best side up depends on the blade direction. Test on a scrap first and check the face and the locking edge for chips.
