Guide · Is vinyl okay for my room?

Before you buy

Is vinyl okay for my room?

Luxury vinyl plank is a reasonable floor in a lot of homes. It is not the right answer for every room, every lease, or every leak. These are the usual worries, in plain terms.

Water, bathrooms, and kitchens

Click-lock LVP is sold as waterproof because the plank itself does not swell the way wood or many laminates do when they get wet. That claim is about the plank, not the room.

Water can still sit in the seams and work down to the subfloor. A floating floor does not seal the plywood or the slab under it. A bathroom or kitchen is a common place for this floor when spills get wiped up. It is not a substitute for fixing a leak. A slow drip under a sink, a failed toilet seal, or standing water can still hurt the subfloor, the walls, and the warranty.

Pets and kids

Yes, if the wear layer matches the traffic. Scratches come from grit, claws, and dragged toys. The number on the box that matters here is the wear layer, in mils. A mil is a thousandth of an inch. It is the thickness of the clear coat on top of the printed picture, not the thickness of the whole plank.

  • 12 mil is a light-use floor. Fine for a quiet bedroom. A weak pick if dogs, kids, and daily traffic are the plan.
  • 20 mil and up is the usual pick for dogs and heavy traffic.

A thicker wear layer slows scratches in the print. It does not make a thin plank feel solid, and it will not hide a rough subfloor.

Renters

Usually not. Click-lock vinyl is a permanent install. It covers the floor that is already there, needs a gap at the walls, and often means taking off baseboards or adding transitions. Taking it back out later is a real job, and many landlords do not want a new floor they did not choose.

Get permission in writing before you buy a box. If you need to leave the place the way you found it, this is the wrong product.

Resale

A wood-look vinyl floor is fine for resale when it looks like wood and the install is clean: straight lines, tight seams, and transitions that sit flush. What hurts is a cheap, thin floor — waves that show through, gapped ends, and a shiny plastic look in a room that should read as wood. Match the look to the house, and do not skip the prep.

If the room is dry enough, the wear layer fits how you live, and you are allowed to leave the floor in place, vinyl plank is a reasonable choice. If any of those three is a no, pause before you order.
