Chapter 3

Is vinyl okay for my room?

Luxury vinyl plank is a reasonable floor in a lot of homes. It is not the right answer for every room, every lease, or every leak. These are the usual worries, in plain terms.

Water, bathrooms, and kitchens

Click-lock LVP is sold as waterproof because the plank itself does not swell the way wood or many laminates do when they get wet. That claim is about the plank, not the room.

Water can still sit in the seams and work down to the subfloor. A floating floor does not seal the plywood or the slab under it. A bathroom or kitchen is a common place for this floor when spills get wiped up. It is not a substitute for fixing a leak. A slow drip under a sink, a failed toilet seal, or standing water can still hurt the subfloor, the walls, and the warranty.

Wear layer

The wear layer is the clear coat on top of the printed wood picture. It is what claws, grit, and shoes actually touch. Boxes list it in mils. One mil is a thousandth of an inch. That number is not the thickness of the whole plank. A 6 mm plank can still have a thin wear layer.

  • About 6 mil is light use. Guest rooms and low traffic. Scratches show sooner.
  • About 12 mil is the usual home floor. Fine for kids and a normal living room if grit gets swept up.
  • 20 mil and up is the pick for dogs, heavy traffic, and rooms that take a beating.

A thicker wear layer slows scratches in the print. It does not make the floor waterproof, it does not make a thin plank feel solid, and it will not hide a rough subfloor. If the label only gives total thickness in millimeters, ask for the wear layer in mils before you buy.

Plank size

The two sizes that change the job are width and length. Thickness is a separate choice about how the floor feels underfoot. Common home planks are about 6 to 7 inches wide and around 48 inches long. Wider boards run about 9 inches. Longer boards run about 60 inches.

  • A wider plank covers a room in fewer rows. A longer plank needs fewer end joints. Fewer pieces means fewer clicks, so the install is faster. That matters most in a big open room.
  • Small rooms (a bathroom, a closet, a narrow hall) usually look better with a narrower, shorter plank. A wide long board can look oversized in a tiny room, and you cut more of it to waste.
  • A large living room or open plan usually looks calmer, and goes down faster, with a wider longer plank. The floor does not look as busy, and you lock fewer boards.
  • A long hallway looks less chopped up if the planks run long in the direction you walk.

Plank size does not change waterproofing or the wear layer. Pick the wear layer for traffic, and the plank size for how the room looks and how long the install takes.

Pets and kids

Yes, if the wear layer matches the traffic. Scratches come from grit, claws, and dragged toys. For dogs and daily traffic, start at 20 mil. A 12 mil floor can still work in a busy home when the clear coat is intact and sand does not sit on it. Mats at the doors matter more than the brand name.

Renters

Usually not. Click-lock vinyl is a permanent install. It covers the floor that is already there, needs a gap at the walls, and often means taking off baseboards or adding transitions. Taking it back out later is a real job, and many landlords do not want a new floor they did not choose.

Get permission in writing before you buy a box. If you need to leave the place the way you found it, this is the wrong product.

Resale

A wood-look vinyl floor is fine for resale when it looks like wood and the install is clean: straight lines, tight seams, and transitions that sit flush. What hurts is a cheap, thin floor — waves that show through, gapped ends, and a shiny plastic look in a room that should read as wood. Match the look to the house, and do not skip the prep.

If the room is dry enough, the wear layer fits how you live, and you are allowed to leave the floor in place, vinyl plank is a reasonable choice. If any of those three is a no, pause before you order.
