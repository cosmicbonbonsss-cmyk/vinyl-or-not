    Quick answer

    LVP vs laminate: which floor fits your room?

    Vinyl plank and laminate look alike in the box and click together the same way. They are not the same floor. Here is how they differ, and which one fits each room.

    The short answer

    Both are wood-look floors that usually click together and float over the subfloor. The big difference is the core. Vinyl plank has a vinyl-based core that does not swell when it gets wet. Standard laminate has a wood-fiber core that can swell if water gets into the seams or edges. That one difference decides most rooms.

    Vinyl plank (LVP)

    A clear wear layer over a printed image, on a rigid (SPC or WPC) or flexible vinyl core. The plank shrugs off spills. Scratch resistance depends on the wear layer, listed in mils.

    Laminate

    A hard clear top over a printed image, on a wood-fiber core. The top is usually very hard. The core is the weak spot when water sits on the floor or gets into the joints.

    Room by room

    Bathrooms & laundry

    Vinyl plank is the usual pick. Water on the floor is normal in these rooms.

    Set on laminate? Only one rated for wet rooms, and read its warranty.

    Kitchens

    Vinyl plank handles spills and mopping better. Laminate can work if spills get wiped up fast.

    Either way, neither floor fixes a leaking dishwasher or sink.

    Basements

    Vinyl plank is the usual pick over concrete that may get damp.

    Test the slab and use the vapor barrier the brand calls for.

    Bedrooms & living rooms

    Either works in a dry room. Pick on look, feel, and the specific product.

    With dogs, look for a 20 mil wear layer on LVP or a hard-wearing laminate.

    Feel, sound, and scratches

    • Underfoot: vinyl usually feels a little softer. Laminate feels harder and closer to wood.
    • Sound: both can sound hollow or clicky over an uneven subfloor or without the right underlayment. Prep matters more than the category.
    • Scratches: grit and claws scratch both. On vinyl plank, a thicker wear layer helps. On either floor, mats at the doors and felt pads on furniture help most.

    Install and cost

    The install is close to the same: an expansion gap at the walls, a staggered layout, and a tapping block and pull bar to seat the joints. Rigid vinyl planks can often be scored with a utility knife and snapped. Laminate usually needs a saw for every cut, which means more dust.

    Prices overlap a lot. Compare the total per square foot, including underlayment, trim, and transitions, not just the price on the box.

    If water ever touches the floor, choose vinyl plank. If the room stays dry, either floor can be a good choice. Pick the product, not the category.

    LVP vs laminate FAQ

    Is LVP better than laminate?

    Neither is better everywhere. LVP is the usual pick where water is a worry, such as bathrooms, kitchens, laundry rooms, and basements, because the plank does not swell when it gets wet. Laminate is a fair choice in dry rooms like bedrooms and living rooms. Compare the specific products, not just the category.

    Is laminate or vinyl plank better for a bathroom?

    Vinyl plank is usually the safer choice for a bathroom. Standard laminate has a wood-fiber core that can swell if water gets into the seams or edges. Some laminates are sold as water-resistant or waterproof, so if you are set on laminate, read that product's rating and warranty for wet rooms first.

    Is LVP or laminate better for dogs?

    Both can work with dogs. Laminate usually has a very hard top surface. LVP depends on its wear layer, and 20 mil and up is the pick for dogs. With either floor, grit and long claws do most of the damage, so trim nails and put mats at the doors.

    Do you install LVP and laminate the same way?

    Mostly. Both are usually click-lock floating floors with an expansion gap at the walls, and both use a tapping block, pull bar, and spacers. Rigid vinyl planks can often be scored with a utility knife and snapped. Laminate usually needs a saw for every cut.
