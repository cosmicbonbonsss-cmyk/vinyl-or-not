Chapter 4

Tools & materials

You don’t need a full contractor van for a click-lock LVP job. Borrow or buy the basics below, and know when each one actually helps. Choose plank thickness and wear layer for the room (see Understand LVP) before you obsess over gadgets.

Measure, mark, and join

Tapping block

Protects the plank’s locking edge while you close a seam. Light taps only — never hit the click edge with a hammer.

Use: every row. View on Amazon

Rubber mallet

Soft taps through a tapping block to seat a stubborn long joint. Align the planks first, then tap lightly.

Use: stubborn long seams. View on Amazon

Spacers

Hold the manufacturer’s expansion gap at walls and fixed objects until trim covers it.

Use: first row and all perimeter walls until trim goes on. View on Amazon

Pull bar

Hooks a plank’s edge so you can pull it tight where a tapping block won’t fit — end joints and the last row near a wall.

Use: last piece of each row and the final row against the wall. View on Amazon

Tape measure

Measures rooms, plank cuts, offsets, and expansion gaps. Measure before layout and again for every cut — don’t trust a quick eyeball.

Use: every project, especially before ordering boxes and transitions.

Chalk line

Snaps a long, straight reference line so the first rows stay square when walls aren’t.

Use: when walls aren’t square or the first rows need a guide.

Pencil and straightedge

Marks cut lines and keeps scores straight — especially around door jambs, vents, and transitions.

Use: every measured cut; mark the finished side carefully.

Cut and shape planks

Vinyl plank guillotine

Quick, quiet straight cuts on products within its thickness rating. Great for square end cuts when the brand and cutter allow it.

Use: square end cuts on thinner or mid rigid planks; check the cutter’s thickness rating. View on Amazon

Utility knife

Scores flexible vinyl, trims underlayment, and cleans small edges. Take repeated controlled passes with a straightedge — rigid SPC often needs a saw instead.

Use: underlayment cuts and light vinyl; rigid core often needs a saw. View on Amazon

Saws for rigid core

A jigsaw, circular saw, miter saw, or oscillating tool handles rigid SPC/WPC and odd shapes. Use the right blade, clamp the plank, and control dust — an oscillating tool is handy for notches and jambs.

Use: rigid SPC/WPC, cutouts, and notches; wear eye protection and control dust. View on Amazon

Door-jamb undercut tool

Cuts clearance under a door casing so a plank can slide beneath it — cleaner than forcing a complex notch.

Use: door casings and jambs that need a clean slide-under fit. View on Amazon

Power tools for cuts and notches

Rent or borrow these if you only need them once. Rigid SPC often needs a saw; an oscillating multi-tool is the usual DIY pick for door jambs and tight notches.

Compound miter saw

Clean crosscuts and angle cuts on rigid planks when you have a lot of square ends to make. Clamp the plank, use a fine blade, and control dust.

Use: repeated square or angled end cuts on rigid SPC/WPC. View on Amazon

Oscillating multi-tool

The usual DIY pick for undercutting door jambs and carving tight notches without a full saw setup. Go slow and use a wood or multi-material blade.

Use: door jambs, vents, and awkward notches. View on Amazon

Circular saw

Straight rip and cross cuts on rigid core when a guillotine isn’t enough. Support the plank, cut face-down or as the maker recommends, and watch kickback.

Use: long straight cuts and thick rigid planks. View on Amazon

Jigsaw

Curves, L-shaped cutouts, and irregular shapes around vents and cabinets. Use a fine tooth blade and keep the shoe flat so the click edge doesn’t chip.

Use: cutouts, curves, and irregular shapes in vinyl or wood-look planks. View on Amazon

Prep the floor

Shop vac

Pulls grit, sawdust, and loose debris that can telegraph through a floor. Vacuum after demolition, repairs, and every cutting session before planks go down.

Use: after demolition, repairs, and cutting — the subfloor must be clean.

Scraper and flat edge

Knocks down bumps and helps you find low spots or debris before a floating floor goes down. Drag a flat edge across the subfloor first.

Use: before install, to find bumps, debris, and spots that need fixing.

Underlayment

Adds an approved layer for sound and feel when the flooring doesn’t already have one. Only if the instructions allow it — don’t double layers blindly.

Use: when instructions call for a separate pad; never double up blindly. View on Amazon

Vapor barrier

Limits moisture vapor from a concrete slab when the flooring system requires it. Use the film or membrane the brand specifies over slabs and below-grade areas.

Use: slabs and below-grade areas per manufacturer moisture limits.

Moisture meter

Checks moisture in wood or helps screen a subfloor before you cover it. Follow the flooring maker’s limits — or get a professional RH test for concrete.

Use: before ordering big or covering the floor; don’t install over a damp slab.

Protect yourself and finish cleanly

Knee pads

Saves your knees on a long click-lock day — especially on concrete or other hard subfloors.

Use: the whole install day. Non-negotiable for most people. View on Amazon

Gloves and safety glasses

Gloves for sharp edges; glasses for chips and dust. Wear both when cutting, prying up old flooring, or handling adhesives.

Use: whenever cutting, prying, or handling adhesives.

Transition strips

Cover expansion gaps and height changes where LVP meets another surface. Match the right T-molding, reducer, end cap, or stair nose to each doorway and floor boundary.

Use: every doorway and where LVP meets another floor.

Trim pry bar

Lifts baseboards or shoe molding without prying against a finished plank. Label pieces so they go back on the right wall.

Use: careful trim removal; label pieces for reinstall.

Prices, colors, and stock change. Order a sample when you can, and check box square footage against your measure tool.

Buying reminder: a 6 mm box may look like a deal but fails hard in garages and often ages poorly in busy homes. For heavy furniture or rolling chairs, treat ~10 mm as a maybe and prefer 12 mm+ (or 20 mm premium) with a thicker wear layer. Garages usually need a different product category entirely — not standard residential LVP.
