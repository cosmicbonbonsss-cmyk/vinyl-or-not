Chapter 3

Tools & materials

You don’t need a full contractor van. Borrow or buy the basics below, and know when each item earns its keep. Buy flooring thickness and wear layer for the room (see Understand LVP) before you obsess over gadgets.

Measure, mark, and join

Tapping block

Protects the plank’s locking edge while you close a seam. Use it whenever a row needs light taps; never strike the click edge directly.

Use: every row — never hammer directly on the locking edge.

Rubber mallet

Delivers soft taps through a tapping block to seat a stubborn long joint. Use light pressure only after the planks are aligned.

Use: stubborn long seams; light taps only.

Spacers

Hold the manufacturer’s expansion gap at walls and fixed objects. Use them along the first row and perimeter until trim covers the gap.

Use: first row and all perimeter walls until trim goes on.

Pull bar

Hooks a plank’s edge so you can draw it tight where a tapping block will not fit. Use it for end joints and the final row near a wall.

Use: final piece of each row and last row against the wall.

Tape measure

Measures rooms, plank cuts, offsets, and expansion gaps. Use it before layout and again for every cut rather than trusting a quick visual estimate.

Use: every project, especially before ordering boxes and transitions.

Chalk line

Snaps a long, straight reference line across a room. Use it to start square when walls are not straight or when the first rows need a guide.

Use: snap a line if walls aren’t square.

Pencil and straightedge

Marks cut lines and guides clean, straight scores. Use them for every measured cut, especially around door jambs, vents, and transitions.

Use: every measured cut; mark the finished side carefully.

Cut and shape planks

Vinyl plank guillotine

Makes quick, quiet straight cuts on products within its thickness rating. Use it for square end cuts when the plank manufacturer and cutter allow it.

Use: square end cuts on thinner or mid rigid planks; check the cutter’s thickness rating.

Utility knife

Scores flexible vinyl, trims underlayment, and cleans small edges. Use repeated controlled passes with a straightedge; rigid SPC often needs a saw instead.

Use: underlayment cuts and light vinyl; rigid core often needs a saw.

Saws for rigid core

A jigsaw, circular saw, miter saw, or oscillating tool handles rigid SPC/WPC cuts and irregular shapes. Use the right blade, secure the plank, and control dust; an oscillating tool is especially useful for notches and jambs.

Use: rigid SPC/WPC, cutouts, and notches; wear eye protection and control dust.

Door-jamb undercut tool

Creates clearance under a door casing so a plank can slide beneath it. Use it where a neat undercut is safer and cleaner than forcing a complex notch.

Use: door casings and jambs that need a clean slide-under fit.

Prep the floor

Shop vac

Removes grit, sawdust, and loose debris that can telegraph through a floor. Use it after demolition, subfloor repairs, and every cutting session before planks go down.

Use: after demolition, repairs, and cutting; the subfloor must be clean.

Scraper and flat edge

Removes bumps and reveals low spots or debris that can affect a floating floor. Use the flat edge across the subfloor before installation to find places that need correction.

Use: before installation to find bumps, debris, and places needing correction.

Underlayment

Adds an approved layer for sound and feel when the flooring does not already have one. Use it only when the flooring instructions allow it; do not double layers blindly.

Use: when instructions call for separate pad; never double up blindly.

Vapor barrier

Helps limit moisture vapor from a concrete slab when the flooring system requires it. Use the specified film or membrane over slabs and below-grade areas, following the product instructions.

Use: slabs and below-grade areas per manufacturer moisture limits.

Moisture meter

Checks moisture conditions in wood or helps screen a subfloor before installation. Use it before covering the floor, and follow the flooring maker’s limits or arrange a professional RH test for concrete.

Use: before ordering big or covering the floor; don’t install over a damp slab.

Protect yourself and finish cleanly

Knee pads

Cushion knees during long stretches of click-lock installation. Use them for the whole install, especially on concrete or other hard subfloors.

Use: the whole install day. Non-negotiable for most people.

Gloves and safety glasses

Gloves protect hands from sharp edges and glasses protect eyes from chips and dust. Use both while cutting, prying up old flooring, or handling adhesives.

Use: whenever cutting, prying up old flooring, or handling adhesives.

Transition strips

Cover expansion gaps and changes in height where LVP meets another surface. Use the correct T-molding, reducer, end cap, or stair nose at each doorway and floor boundary.

Use: every doorway and where LVP meets another floor.

Trim pry bar

Lifts baseboards or shoe molding without putting force on a finished plank. Use it during careful trim removal and keep trim labeled so it can return to the right wall.

Use: careful trim removal; label pieces so they return to the right wall.
Buying reminder: a 6 mm box may look like a deal but fails hard in garages and often ages poorly in busy homes. For heavy furniture or rolling loads, treat ~10 mm as a maybe and prefer 12 mm+ (or 20 mm premium) with a thicker wear layer. Garages usually need a different product category entirely — not standard residential LVP.
