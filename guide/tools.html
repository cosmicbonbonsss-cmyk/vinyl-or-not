Chapter 3

Tools & materials

You don’t need a full contractor van. Borrow or buy the basics below, and know when each item earns its keep. Buy flooring thickness and wear layer for the room (see Understand LVP) before you obsess over gadgets.

Core install kit (click-lock)

Tapping block

Plastic or wood block that protects the click edge while you tap planks together.

Use: every row — never hammer directly on the locking edge.

Rubber mallet

Soft blows to seat long joints after the tapping block is in place.

Use: stubborn long seams; light taps only.

Spacers

Wedges or clips that hold a consistent expansion gap at walls (often ~¼ in — follow the brand).

Use: first row and all perimeter walls until trim goes on.

Pull bar

Metal bar that hooks the last plank against the wall so you can tap it closed.

Use: final piece of each row and last row against the wall.

Vinyl / laminate cutter (guillotine)

Shears straight cuts on many LVP planks without a power saw — quiet and fast.

Use: square end cuts on thinner/mid rigid planks; check thickness rating of the cutter.

Utility knife + straightedge

Score-and-snap for some flexible products; trim underlayment; clean edges.

Use: underlayment cuts; light vinyl. Rigid SPC often needs a saw instead.

Jigsaw / oscillating multi-tool

Cuts notches for door jambs, vents, and irregular shapes in rigid core.

Use: rigid SPC/WPC, cutouts, undercut jambs (oscillating tool shines here).

Tape measure + pencil + chalk line

Layout, cut marks, and a straight reference line for the first rows.

Use: every project — snap a line if walls aren’t square.

Knee pads

Comfort and fewer bruised knees on hard subfloors.

Use: the whole install day. Non-negotiable for most people.

Safety glasses + gloves

Eye protection for cutting; gloves for handling edges and adhesive if any.

Use: whenever cutting or demo’ing old flooring.

Materials & finishing pieces

Underlayment

Foam or cork layer for sound/feel — only if not already attached, and only if the brand allows it.

Use: when instructions call for separate pad; never double up blindly.

Vapor barrier

Plastic film or approved membrane over concrete when required.

Use: slabs and below-grade per manufacturer moisture limits.

Transition strips

T-moldings, reducers, end caps, stair nosing — cover expansion gaps at doorways and height changes.

Use: every doorway and where LVP meets other floors.

Moisture basics

Moisture meter or pro RH test on concrete; don’t install over a damp slab.

Use: before ordering big — failures here void warranties.
Buying reminder: a 6 mm box may look like a deal but fails hard in garages and often ages poorly in busy homes. For heavy furniture or rolling loads, treat ~10 mm as a maybe and prefer 12 mm+ (or 20 mm premium) with a thicker wear layer. Garages usually need a different product category entirely — not standard residential LVP.
