    Finish, clean & care for vinyl plank floors

    Once the planks are down, trim, transitions, and gentle cleaning make the floor look finished — and help it last.

    Finish the edges

    • Remove spacers; reinstall baseboards or add matching quarter-round. Nail into the wall, not the floating floor.
    • Set transition strips so they cover the expansion gap but don’t pin the floor tightly.
    • Caulk only where the brand allows (often wall/trim junctions — not locking seams).

    First-week care

    • Furniture: lift, don’t drag. Use felt pads.
    • Rolling chairs: use a floor mat rated for vinyl.
    • Clean up grit — sand acts like sandpaper on wear layers.

    How to clean vinyl plank flooring

    • Soft broom or vacuum (no beater bar digging into embossing).
    • Damp mop with cleaner approved for vinyl — avoid steam mops and soaking water.
    • Skip wax/polish unless the manufacturer says otherwise (most modern wear layers don’t want it).
    Keep one sealed carton of leftover planks for future repairs. Dye lots change.

    Cleaning & care FAQ

    What is the best way to clean vinyl plank flooring?

    Sweep or vacuum grit often, using a hard-floor setting with no spinning beater bar. Then damp mop with a pH-neutral cleaner made for vinyl or the one your flooring brand recommends. Wring the mop well so water doesn't sit at the seams.

    Can you use a steam mop on vinyl plank flooring?

    Most vinyl plank makers say no. Heat and steam can work into the seams and soften or weaken the planks and locks, and using one may void the warranty. Check your brand's care sheet before you try it.

    Can you clean LVP with vinegar?

    Some brands allow a weak vinegar and water mix and others say to use only a pH-neutral vinyl cleaner. Check your care sheet. Avoid wax, oil soaps, abrasive powders, and polishes unless the brand approves them.

    Do you nail baseboards or quarter-round into vinyl plank?

    No. A floating floor has to move, so nail baseboards and quarter-round into the wall, sitting just above the planks. Nailing into the floor pins it and can cause gaps or peaking.

    Do rubber-backed rugs damage vinyl plank floors?

    Some can. Certain rubber and latex backings can stain or discolor vinyl over time. Use rug pads labeled safe for vinyl floors.
