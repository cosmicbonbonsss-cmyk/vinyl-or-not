    Vinyl plank flooring problems and how to fix them

    Most problems that show up after install come from prep, the wrong product for the load, or locks that never fully seated. Check this list before you rip everything out.

    Common vinyl plank flooring problems and fixes

    Gaps between planks

    Locks not fully engaged, missing spacers causing binding, or seasonal movement without a gap at the walls.

    Try: reseat with tapping block; verify perimeter expansion; check for pinched trim.

    Peaking / tenting at seams

    Often moisture under the floor, no expansion gap, or floor forced against walls/cabinets.

    Try: stop mopping wet; inspect gaps; moisture test slab — call a pro if widespread.

    Won’t click / broken lock

    Debris in the joint, wrong angle, damaged tongue, or product too thin for the abuse.

    Try: clean groove; replace damaged plank; don’t force with a steel hammer on the edge.

    Telegraphing / ridges show

    Subfloor not flat; thin LVP highlighting every ripple.

    Fix: leveling before reinstall — thicker product helps only within brand flatness limits.

    Dents & early wear

    Wear layer too thin, missing chair mats, grit, or planks too thin for furniture/rolling loads.

    Prevent: pads, mats, thicker wear layer next time (see Understand LVP).

    Hollow / clicky sound

    Uneven subfloor, missing underlayment where required, or plank not seated.

    Try: map high/low spots; confirm pad requirements.
    Call a pro when: moisture readings fail, large areas peak, mold/odor appears, radiant heat is involved, or stairs/bathrooms need waterproofing details you’re unsure about.

    Vinyl plank problems FAQ

    Why are my vinyl plank seams separating?

    Usually because the click joints never fully locked, the floor is pinned at the edges by trim, cabinets, or a missing expansion gap, or the room has big temperature swings, such as strong sun through a window. Tap the planks back together with a tapping block and check that nothing is holding the floor down at the walls.

    Why is my LVP peaking or buckling?

    Peaking at the seams usually means the floor has no room to move. The expansion gap is missing, or baseboards, a heavy island, or transition strips are pinning it. Moisture under the floor can cause it too. Free up the edges first. If the floor is wet underneath or the problem is widespread, call a pro.

    Why does my vinyl plank floor sound hollow?

    A little hollow sound is normal for a floating floor. A loud or spongy spot usually means a dip in the subfloor, so the plank bridges a gap, or a plank that is not fully seated. Fixing it properly usually means lifting the planks and filling the low spot.

    Can you replace one damaged vinyl plank?

    Yes. If it is near a wall, you can unclick the rows back to it and swap the plank. In the middle of a room, a common fix is to cut out the damaged plank, trim the locking edge off the replacement, and glue it in. Keep a box of spare planks from the same lot for this.
