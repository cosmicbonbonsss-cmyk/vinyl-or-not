    Guide · LVP underlayment

    Quick answer

    Do you need underlayment for vinyl plank flooring?

    Often, no. Most click-lock vinyl plank sold today has a pad already attached, and the maker does not want a second one. The big exception is concrete: many brands want a 6 mil plastic vapor barrier under the planks even when they have a pad. Here is how to tell what your floor needs.

    The short version

    Your situationWhat usually goes under the planks
    Planks with attached pad, wood subfloorNothing extra. Install on the clean, flat subfloor.
    Planks with attached pad, concrete slab6 mil poly vapor barrier, seams overlapped and taped. No extra cushion.
    Planks with no padA thin, dense underlayment the brand approves. On concrete, one with a built-in vapor barrier, or film plus pad, as the sheet says.
    Glue-down planksNo underlayment and no plastic film. The adhesive bonds to the prepared subfloor.
    Condo or upstairs room with sound rulesAn acoustic underlayment that meets the building's rating, if the plank maker allows it.

    These are common rules, not universal ones. Every brand prints its own list of approved underlayments, and using the wrong one is an easy way to lose the warranty. Find the install sheet for your exact product (it is usually a PDF on the product page) before you buy anything to go under it.

    What an attached pad does, and what it doesn't

    The thin foam or cork layer on the back of many planks softens the feel, takes the edge off the hollow click when you walk, and covers tiny bumps in the subfloor. It does not block moisture from a concrete slab, and it does not make an uneven floor flat. That is why brands that sell padded planks still ask for plastic film on concrete and still list a flatness limit.

    Why you shouldn't stack a second pad

    Rigid core vinyl holds together with thin click joints. When there is too much cushion underneath, the plank sinks a little every time someone steps on a seam. Over months that flexing can open gaps, crack the locks, or make seams peak. Many install sheets say plainly: no additional underlayment over the attached pad. A few allow one thin, dense layer for sound. If yours does, it will name the product or the maximum thickness.

    Concrete slabs and vapor barriers

    1. Test first. Tape a square of plastic to the slab for a couple of days, or use the test your brand names. Damp underneath means stop and solve the moisture before any floor goes down. The basement guide covers the tests.
    2. Lay 6 mil polyethylene film across the whole slab, unless your planks' approved underlayment already has a vapor barrier built in.
    3. Overlap the seams by the amount the sheet asks (often about 8 in) and tape them with moisture-resistant tape.
    4. Run it slightly up the wall and trim it after the baseboard or quarter-round covers the edge.
    5. Lay planks on top the same way as on any other floor. The film is not a cushion and does not count as an extra pad.

    Over a plywood or OSB subfloor, most brands do not want plastic film, because it can trap moisture in the wood. Follow the sheet.

    Picking an underlayment when you do need one

    • Thin and dense. Rigid core vinyl usually wants about 1 to 1.5 mm, with some brands allowing up to 2 or 3 mm. Thick laminate foam is too soft for most vinyl plank.
    • Made for vinyl. The package should say it works under LVP or rigid core. Carpet pad never goes under vinyl plank.
    • Vapor barrier built in if it is going over concrete and the brand allows it in place of film.
    • Sound rating (IIC and STC numbers) if your HOA or condo board has rules. Ask them for the required numbers before you buy.

    Underlayment won't fix: dips, humps, or a squeaky subfloor. Those get fixed in prep before anything goes down. A soft pad over a dip lets the planks flex and is a common cause of broken locks.

    LVP underlayment FAQ

    Do you need underlayment for vinyl plank flooring?

    Not always. Most rigid core (SPC or WPC) click-lock planks sold today have a pad attached, and the maker usually says not to add another cushion layer. Planks without a pad often need a thin underlayment the maker approves. Glue-down planks get no underlayment at all. The install sheet for your exact product is the final word.

    Can you put underlayment under LVP that already has an attached pad?

    Usually not a second cushion layer. Stacking foam under a padded plank lets the floor flex, which can strain the click joints and void the warranty. A few brands allow a thin, dense extra layer, for example for sound in a condo, so check the brand's approved list before you buy one.

    Do I need a vapor barrier for LVP on concrete?

    Usually yes. Many brands require 6 mil polyethylene film over a concrete slab, on any level of the house, even when the planks have an attached pad. The pad cushions the floor but does not stop moisture coming up from the slab. Overlap the seams, tape them, and run the film a little up the wall, as your install sheet says.

    Do I need a vapor barrier over a plywood subfloor?

    Usually not. Most brands do not want plastic film over a wood subfloor, because it can trap moisture in the wood. Some allow or ask for a specific breathable underlayment instead. Follow your product's sheet.

    Can underlayment fix an uneven subfloor?

    No. Underlayment is for sound and feel, not for leveling. Soft or thick pads let the planks flex over dips, which can break the locks. Fix high spots and fill low spots first so the floor meets the brand's flatness limit, often about 3/16 inch over 10 feet.

    What thickness of underlayment is best for LVP?

    Thin and firm. Rigid core vinyl wants a dense pad, often around 1 to 1.5 mm, with some brands allowing up to about 2 to 3 mm. Thick, squishy foam made for laminate or carpet pad is the wrong choice for most vinyl plank.
