    Prep the subfloor for vinyl plank

    Get the subfloor flat, dry, and clean before you lay the first plank. Click-lock forgives small mistakes in tapping — it does not forgive a wavy, wet, or dirty subfloor. Prep is where DIY jobs succeed or fail.

    Clear and protect

    • Remove baseboards carefully if you plan to reuse them (label pieces).
    • Haul out furniture; protect adjacent rooms from dust.
    • Decide whether old flooring stays (only if the manufacturer allows it — and it’s flat, sound, and thin enough). Covering tile? See Can you put LVP over tile?

    Flat, dry, clean

    1. Flatness — many brands want roughly 3/16 in over 10 ft (confirm your sheet). Grind high spots; fill low spots with leveling compound rated for your substrate.
    2. Moisture — concrete slabs need proper testing (surface “looks dry” is not enough). Wood subfloors should be sound, screwed down, and within moisture limits.
    3. Clean — scrape adhesive ridges, sweep, vacuum. Debris telegraphs through thin LVP.
    4. Underlayment / vapor barrier — install only what the brand specifies. Skip a second pad if foam is already attached.
    5. Acclimate — follow the box: some rigid cores need little time; others want 48 hours in the room.
    Red flags — pause: soft/spongy spots, musty smell, mold, large cracks with height change, recent flooding without a documented dry-out. Get a pro opinion before you open boxes.

    Underlayment & prep FAQ

    What is the best underlayment for LVP?

    The one your plank maker specifies. If the planks already have a pad attached, most brands do not want a second pad under them. If they do not, use a thin, dense underlayment made for vinyl plank. Thick soft foam or carpet pad lets the locks flex and can break them.

    Do I need a vapor barrier under LVP on concrete?

    Often, yes. Many brands call for a plastic vapor barrier over a concrete slab unless the attached pad already includes one. Test the slab for moisture either way, and follow the install sheet for your exact product.

    How flat does a subfloor need to be for LVP?

    Many brands want roughly 3/16 inch over 10 feet, but confirm the number on your install sheet. Grind high spots, fill low spots, and check again with a long straightedge before you open boxes.

    Can you put LVP over tile?

    Often, yes, if the tile is flat and solid and the brand allows it. Wide grout lines usually need to be filled first. See Can you put LVP over tile? for the full checklist.
