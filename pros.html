Replace vs spot-repair (LVP)

  • Spot-repair when a few planks are damaged, dye lot matches attic stock, and the click system still locks cleanly.
  • Replace the room / unit when wear-through is widespread, seams are peaking, moisture is under the floor, or matching product is discontinued.
  • LVP is generally not refinished like hardwood — plan replace, not sand-and-coat.

Turnover walkthrough photo tips

  • Wide shot of each room + close-ups of seams, transitions, and wet areas (kitchen, bath, patio door)
  • Note unit number and date on your notes; photos stay local in this prototype
  • Flag soft spots, musty odors, and appliance leaks before calling for install bids

Rental moisture / red-flag checklist

  • Recurring leaks (sinks, dishwashers, water heaters)
  • Soft or stained flooring at exterior doors and tub surrounds
  • Musty closets on slabs — see moisture-testing-notes.txt
  • Document before/after on turnovers when replacing floors

Designers

Specs, looks & client presentation aids

Downloadable planning aids, links to the photo overlay and texture gallery, and finish notes you can hand to clients — without claiming manufacturer authority.

Visual references

  • LVP floor overlay — upload a room photo, tap four corners, preview seamless plank textures
  • Texture set: warm oak, gray oak, walnut, whitewash, wide plank, and more (CC0 via ambientCG) — pick looks for mood boards
  • Known limits: manual corners, no AI floor detection, lighting not matched — set client expectations

Finish / look notes for presentations

  • Call out wear-layer mils vs core thickness separately (clients often conflate them)
  • Note waterproof language carefully — plank waterproof ≠ flood-proof subfloor
  • Direction of lay and pattern (straight vs diagonal) change waste % — see waste-factor guide
  • Print the planning aid HTML for job folders
