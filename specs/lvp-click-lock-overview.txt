LVP click-lock overview — Vinyl or Not (planning aid)
======================================================
Disclaimer: Planning aid only — not a manufacturer specification sheet
or warranty. Confirm product data, install instructions, and warranties
with the brand and your installer before ordering.

Wear layer
----------
- Measured in mils (thousandths of an inch). Higher mils generally mean
  better scratch and wear resistance for traffic.
- Residential: often 6–12 mil; light commercial / busy homes: 12–20+ mil.
- Wear layer is a clear protective coat over the printed design layer —
  it is not the core thickness.

Core
----
- Rigid-core (SPC / WPC) products resist dents better than flexible glue-down
  or loose-lay vinyl in many installs.
- Thickness (e.g. 4–8 mm total) affects underfoot feel and sound; it is
  separate from wear-layer mils.
- Attached underlayment pads vary — check compatibility with radiant heat
  and moisture barriers.

Waterproof claims (read the fine print)
---------------------------------------
- "Waterproof" usually means the plank itself resists water; it does not
  always mean flood-proof or that water under the floor is fine.
- Edges, transitions, and subfloor still matter. Standing water can damage
  adjacent materials and trapped moisture under floating floors.
- Always follow manufacturer moisture limits for concrete slabs.

Install notes (generic)
-----------------------
- Click-lock = floating floor in most cases; leave expansion gaps at walls.
- Subfloor should be clean, dry, and flat within manufacturer tolerances.
- Acclimate product per brand instructions when required.
- Stagger end joints; avoid short pieces at doorways when possible.
- Use approved underlayment / vapor barriers only.
