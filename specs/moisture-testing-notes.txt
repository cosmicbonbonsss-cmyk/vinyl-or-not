Moisture testing notes — Vinyl or Not (planning aid)
====================================================
Disclaimer: Planning aid only — not a manufacturer specification sheet,
lab procedure, or warranty. Moisture failures are a leading cause of
flooring claims — use qualified testers and brand-approved methods.

Why it matters
--------------
Trapped moisture under floating LVP/laminate can cause peaking, cupping,
mold risk at edges, and voided warranties even when the plank is labeled
"waterproof."

Common approaches (confirm with manufacturer)
---------------------------------------------
- Concrete: in-situ relative humidity (RH) probes (e.g. ASTM F2170) and/or
  calcium chloride (ASTM F1869) — brand docs specify which and the limits.
- Wood subfloors: pin or pinless meters; compare to ambient RH and
  manufacturer max moisture content %.
- Always record date, weather, HVAC status, and test locations on a sketch.

Practical tips for pros & property managers
-------------------------------------------
- Test after the building is enclosed and HVAC is running (when required).
- Retest after major water events or before multi-unit turnover installs.
- Do not rely on "it looks dry" or a single surface reading.
- If readings exceed limits: mitigate (dry, seal, barrier) before install —
  do not hope the floor will "breathe it out."

Rental / turnover red flags
---------------------------
- Recurring leaks under sinks, dishwashers, water heaters
- Soft spots at patio doors or tub surrounds
- Musty closets on slabs
- Recent flooding without documented dry-out
